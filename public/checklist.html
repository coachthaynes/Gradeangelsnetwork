The Rubric and Time Saving Grading Checklist

Cut grading time without cutting feedback quality.

12 habits that save grading time

  1. Grade with the key open, not in your headWrite the answer key before the first paper. Deciding as you go is the slowest part of grading.
  2. Decide what you will not markPick the two or three skills this assignment is really about and give feedback on those only.
  3. Grade one question across every paperQuestion 1 on all papers, then question 2. You stay consistent and get faster with each paper.
  4. Use a comment bankKeep your ten most common comments numbered, then write the number. Share the bank with students.
  5. Set a timer per paperThree minutes for a short response, eight for an essay. The timer keeps you from over marking.
  6. Sort before you startSkim and stack papers into strong, middle, and needs support. Grading within a pile is quicker and fairer.
  7. Mark the first error, not every errorCircle the first instance of a repeated mistake and note "check for this throughout."
  8. Let students self check firstA quick checklist students complete before turning work in removes easy errors from your pile.
  9. Collect less, check more oftenSpot check homework for completion and fully grade one problem. Save deep grading for key assessments.
  10. Stop at the halfway pointGrade half the stack, take a real break, then finish. Accuracy drops sharply after an hour.
  11. Protect student privacyUse ID numbers instead of names on work you share or photograph, and keep graded work off shared screens.
  12. Hand off what you do not need to grade yourselfObjective work like quizzes and worksheets can be graded by someone vetted with your key, so your time goes to feedback that matters.
